<p> I mainly make these graphics thanks to, and with the help of Adobe.</p>
<p> I start in <b>Illustrator</b> or <b>Photoshop</b>. Two completely different programs (pixels and vectors and stuff). Each has their own strengths, but I often aim for their weaknesses. By moving an image from one program environment to the other very often, these strengths tend to disappear completely.</p> <p> Pixels become vectors and vice versa, countless times, back and forth. I like to edit and copy until my computer stops responding. Then he asks if he can restart or if he can give the program some time to get back to his senses. Usually, the computer does come through a little later. Save and save again is the message.</p><p> Usually this process generates something that I find fascinating: <b>noise</b>.</p><p>The software feels abused and spits out new shapes and textures. Then things get interesting. </p> <p>The title of this series is a silent, modest wink to &quot;A Universe from Nothing: Why there is something rather than nothing&quot;, by <b>Lawrence Krauss</b>.</p>
