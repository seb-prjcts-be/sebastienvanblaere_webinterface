<h3>Entropy</h3>
<p style='text-align:justify;'>Entropy is a fundamental concept in thermodynamics and physics, describing the degree of disorder or uncertainty within a closed system. It is intrinsically linked to the second law of thermodynamics, which states that the total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease; it can only increase or remain constant. This process represents a natural tendency of systems to move from a state of low entropy (order, concentration) to a state of high entropy (disorder, dispersion).</p>
<p style='text-align:justify;'>A classic example is a cup of hot tea cooling in a room: the heat dissipates, energy becomes evenly distributed, and the system's entropy increases.</p>
<p style='text-align:justify;'>Entropy is often seen as an explanation for the arrow of time. While many physical processes appear reversible, the irreversible increase of entropy makes time unidirectional. In everyday life, this is evident in aging, the decay of structures, and the dissipation of energy. According to some scientific theories, the universe itself is moving toward a state of thermal death: a condition in which everything is evenly distributed and no energy differences remain to perform work.</p>
