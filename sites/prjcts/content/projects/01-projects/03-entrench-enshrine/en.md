<p>By superimposing my own photography with found medical imagery, I create a new visual layer that intertwines personal experiences with universal themes.</p>

<h3>Entrench:</h3>
<p>To establish something firmly so that it cannot be changed; anchored in time and space.</p>

<h3>Enshrine:</h3>
<p>To contain or keep in a place that is highly admired and respected; sanctified by its context.</p><br>

<p>The elements from the medical imagery, and particularly the white spaces between them, form unexpected graphical lines and patterns. These lines cut through the composition, isolating parts of the image into random segments, creating a sense of fragmentation and dissection. They symbolize always carrying my medical conditions with me wherever I go, whatever I do—unseen but ever-present.</p>

<p>These medical elements add a deeper layer to the work, blurring the boundaries between the personal and the universal. The white spaces serve not only as aesthetic components but also as metaphors for the voids and uncertainties that come with living with a chronic condition.</p>

<p>By 'entrenching' and 'enshrining' my work, I aim to anchor these experiences and create a safe space where they can be appreciated and respected. It is an attempt to acknowledge the unchangeable nature of my condition and give it a place within my artistic expression.</p>

<p>The superimposition of images creates a dialogue between visibility and invisibility, between what is shown and what remains hidden. It invites the viewer to look beyond the surface and discover the underlying layers of meaning.</p>

<p>In this way, the personal becomes universal, and my individual experience gains broader relevance. The work serves as a bridge between my inner world and the external world, encouraging reflection and empathy in the viewer.</p>
